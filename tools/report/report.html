01 — Project overview

Every line in English, in the actor's own voice, on the actor's own lips.

The objective

Convert the first episode of Rise of the Raven into American English for an international audience. Every character should keep the tone and character of the original actor's voice, and every on-camera line should be lip-synced so the English reads as if it had been performed on set.

The material

58 picture-locked clips of the episode (1920×1080, 23.976 fps) with their dialogue tracks. Most of the episode is in Hungarian, but the scenes with the Serbian lord Branković and his family are performed in Serbian, so the work covered two source languages.

What we accomplished

  • All 58 clips delivered in American English, in the original order and with the original file names (plus an _EN suffix).
  • Each speaking role voiced with a clone of its own actor's performance. Six principal characters received dedicated, purpose-built voice models.
  • Lip sync on every shot where the speaker's face is visible, pinned to that speaker only. Shots with two people talking were handled in two passes.
  • Every line checked against the original and corrected where needed: names, period vocabulary and meaning.
  • Picture untouched wherever no lip sync was needed. Every file is frame-exact to its source.

Our approach

Off-the-shelf dubbing gets a scene roughly right. Our custom workflow adds the precision a drama needs: it identifies who is speaking in every frame, keeps each character's voice consistent across the episode, verifies the English line by line against the original, and only alters the picture where the speaker's mouth is actually on screen.

04 — Stage 2 · Speaker identification

Who is speaking, frame by frame

A lip-sync model will animate whatever face it is given. The precision comes from deciding, line by line, whose mouth should move and whose should not.

Lip-motion analysis

Mouth opening is tracked per face and correlated with the speech in the dialogue track.

Voice analysis

Pitch and voice-identity analysis of each sentence separate male, female and child speakers, and tell characters apart.

Word-level gating

Each lip-sync pass hears only its speaker's words, timed to the individual word, so a reply never moves the wrong mouth.

06 — Stages 3 & 4 · Dubbing, script verification & correction

Line-by-line verification, down to a single word

Dubbing with voice cloning

The episode is dubbed as one continuous performance rather than clip by clip. Each character keeps the same cloned voice throughout, and the English is level-matched to the original dialogue so the mix sits exactly where the original did.

Script verification

We transcribe both the original dialogue and the English dub, then compare them line by line. Mistranslated lines, mispronounced names and missing lines are corrected in the character's own dedicated voice model, timed to the actor's original delivery.

09 — Stage 6 · Lip sync

The English on the actor's lips

Our lip-sync model re-renders only the lower face of the speaking actor, shot by shot, so the mouth forms the English words. Skin, beard, lighting, grain and the rest of the frame stay as photographed.

Shot-trimmed

Each pass covers exactly one shot, cut on the frame, so a lip-sync never runs across an edit.

Face-pinned

Each pass is anchored to the speaker's face on a chosen frame, verified against face detection.

Speaker-gated

The model hears only the on-screen speaker's own words, so listeners don't move their lips.

Dual-pass two-shots

When two people talk in one shot, each gets their own pass, chained on top of the other.
